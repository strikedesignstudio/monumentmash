Monument Mashup

For over 100 years the statues on Deptford Town Hall have projected a colonial perspective. Visit new monuments imagined by local young people by clicking the objects on the screen and listening to experiences, music, stories and poetry that challenge colonial history in the present and share important practices of resistance.

Click on the floating monuments to explore different audio segments and watch them drift through space.

Controls

  • Click & Drag: Rotate the camera view
  • Scroll: Zoom in and out
  • Click Monuments: Jump to audio segments
  • Play/Pause: Control audio playback

Monument Mash Up is an interactive website, where the statues on Deptford Town Hall are re-imagined to challenge the legacies of colonisation and enslavement they signify. Developed through a unique workshop series including art, architecture, design, and theories drawn from the Black Radical Tradition, young people from Lewisham collaborated with artist Dele Adeyemo, community researchers from Deptford People’s Heritage Museum and academics from Goldsmiths BA Curating to generate 3D models. Drawing from practices of Black artistry, and community-led architecture and curating, these models bridge the colonial past and their experiences of Deptford today.

Deptford Town Hall is a Grade II listed building built in 1905 and acquired by Goldsmiths in 2000. The statues on its facade represent a colonial history that links to Transatlantic enslavement, plantation profiteering and racial injustice. In 2019, Goldsmiths Anti-Racist Action (GARA) held a 137-day sit-in occupation of the building, protesting inaction on racial injustice at the College. The occupation was brought to a close on 27 July 2019 after the College management and the campaigners established a mutually-agreed statement of commitments. One of these commitments was to allow greater community access and engagement with Deptford Town Hall. The occupation is an inspiration for local young people fighting for changes in the area, and it is in this legacy that Monumental Mash Up was born.

Part of the Deptford People’s Heritage Museum / Goldsmiths’ The Monument is the Struggle, this project attempts to answer the question what comes after the monument? Controversies over statues in the public realm often focus more on symbolic dimensions of the past and less on the persistence of dispossession in the present. We understand the monument to be a remnant of something more collective and relational, something that can be used to both contest problematic histories and generate resistant futures.
